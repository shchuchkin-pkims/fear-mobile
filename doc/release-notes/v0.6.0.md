## F.E.A.R. Messenger v0.6.0 for Android

This release brings Android level with the desktop on Phases C–F of the [roadmap](https://github.com/shchuchkin-pkims/fear/wiki/Roadmap): the room key follows the room, the relay no longer learns room names or display names, calls work for groups, and messages reach contacts who are offline – with notifications that still need nothing from Google.

> **Not compatible with v0.5.x.** Update every device **and the relay** together.

### Encryption

- **The room key rotates on every change of membership**, speaking the same bytes as the desktop (pinned by shared test vectors on both sides). A newcomer cannot read what was said before; a member who left cannot read what comes after.
- Chat is sealed under per-epoch keys; during a change two generations are kept, so nothing in flight is lost.
- **Calls with per-sender keys**, authenticated announcements, and a replay window per sender – Android had no replay protection for calls before.
- Key delivery (ECDH) requires a verified signature; key material is never logged.
- Encrypted contact blobs can only be read by their owner (signed one-shot challenge).
- **One fingerprint per key on every platform**: the short `name#xxxxxxxx` is now the start of the full fingerprint, as on the desktop. Trusted keys saved under the old short form are renamed once, keeping their "verified" mark.

### Privacy

- The relay sees a hash instead of the room name and a fresh random tag per connection instead of your name; calls use the same tags.
- Personal chats get identifiers the relay cannot compute; history moves over on first connect.
- **Notifications stay free of Google services:** the background service now also checks the offline inbox (less often in the background than on screen), and the app draws the notification itself. Google learns neither when you get messages nor that you do.
- The lock screen now shows "New message" – no text, no sender.

### Messaging

- **Offline inbox:** messages to a contact who is not online wait on the relay, sealed, and are collected when they come back – even while you sit in another room.
- **The database no longer wipes your history** on a schema change: explicit migrations, checked against the exported schema.
- The member count of a room; a date above the first message of each day; a failed join says which way it failed.

### Calls

- **Group voice** (a decoder per participant, mixed) and **group video** – one person large, everyone in a strip, names under the tiles.
- An **incoming call screen**; a video call can be answered from the invitation; a call stays up while unanswered.
- **Video adapts to the network:** the bitrate drops when the upload cannot keep up and returns to your setting when it clears; the frame-rate setting is now a real limit (a phone set to 20 sent 31 frames a second before); CBR where the encoder supports it. On a live call over Wi-Fi the upload fell from 2.6 to about 1.8 Mbit/s and the share of frames the other side could show rose.
- Microphone sensitivity, and the system noise suppressor can be switched off on phones where it cuts quiet speech. Bitrate and frame rate can be set on top of the chosen quality.
- Leaving a video call no longer kills the app – and with it the key wiping.

### Install

Download `fear-android-v0.6.0.apk` below. It is signed with the same key as v0.5.0, so it installs over it and keeps your data; the in-app updater (menu → check for updates) finds it too.

### License

The Android app is now **GPL-3.0-or-later** (previously MIT); the license text ships inside the APK.

### Known Limitations

- Notifications cannot wake an app the system has killed: that takes Firebase Cloud Messaging, which would hand Google the timing of your messages. A deliberate trade.
- The offline inbox is checked periodically, less often in the background.
- Direct calls (STUN) are desktop-only for now; Android calls go through the relay.
- No post-compromise security (no ratchet), as on the desktop.
